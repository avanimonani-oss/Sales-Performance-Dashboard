# Executive Summary

## Performance snapshot

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total revenue | $121,750,194 |
| Total profit | $21,201,060 |
| Orders | 50,000 |
| Average order value | $2,435 |
| Profit margin | 17.4% |
| Active customers | 30 |
| Product catalog | 44 SKUs |

## Findings

**Regional performance** — East generates the strongest revenue and average order value. Central underperforms on revenue per order and profit margin, suggesting pricing, mix, or fulfillment gaps worth a targeted review.

**Category mix** — Technology contributes the largest profit pool with the healthiest margins. Furniture and Office Supplies are more discount-sensitive; deep promotions in those categories erode margin faster than volume gains justify.

**Customer concentration** — The top decile of customers drives over 45% of revenue. With only 30 active accounts in the dataset, retention of key buyers and mid-tier account development should be parallel priorities.

**Seasonality** — Revenue peaks in Q4, consistent with year-end purchasing. A secondary uplift in June points to mid-year procurement cycles that can inform inventory and campaign timing.

## Recommendations

1. **Central region** — Run a margin diagnostic by sub-category; test bundle offers over blanket discounts.
2. **Technology** — Expand high-margin accessories and cross-sell from Furniture and Office Supplies buyers.
3. **Customer risk** — Introduce tiered loyalty for the top 20% while building pipeline in mid-revenue bands.
4. **Discount governance** — Cap discount depth on low-margin SKUs; report weekly on discount-to-profit ratio by category.
5. **Reporting cadence** — Review the executive dashboard monthly against prior year and plan for forecast calibration.

## Expected impact

Tighter discount control and category focus can lift blended margin by 1–2 points without volume loss. Reducing customer concentration lowers revenue volatility. Monthly dashboard reviews align sales, finance, and operations on a single KPI set.
